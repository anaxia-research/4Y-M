# Flow, reflection positivity and retention of a continuum excitation

Research proof note, 18 November 2026. This note supplies general theorems and an explicit counterexample. It does not prove a weak-coupling Wilson bound, a nontrivial Yang–Mills limit or a Clay mass gap. No novelty claim is made for the general mechanisms.

## 1. Full Euclidean heat flow can destroy reflection positivity

Let \(\omega>0\). The stationary real Gaussian process with covariance
\[
C_0(t)=\frac{e^{-\omega|t|}}{2\omega}
=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{ipt}}{p^2+\omega^2}\frac{dp}{2\pi}
\]
is reflection positive: for positive times \(t_j\),
\[
\sum_{i,j}\overline c_i c_j C_0(t_i+t_j)
=\frac1{2\omega}\left|\sum_j c_j e^{-\omega t_j}\right|^2\ge0.
\]
This is one spatial Fourier mode of a massive free scalar field in finite spatial volume. Now apply heat flow in the Euclidean time direction, or full Euclidean heat flow, to both fields. At positive flow time \(s>0\) the corresponding covariance has the form
\[
C_s(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{ipt}
\frac{e^{-2s(p^2+\omega^2)}}{p^2+\omega^2}\frac{dp}{2\pi}.
\]
The additional positive constant \(e^{-2s\omega^2}\) is immaterial for reflection positivity; it is included to display a massive full heat flow. The covariance is an ordinary positive-definite stationary covariance, since its Fourier density is positive. It is even, real and infinitely differentiable, and the heat-flow map commutes with the reflection \(t\mapsto-t\).

**Theorem.** For every \(s>0\), this flowed Gaussian process is not reflection positive with respect to Euclidean time reflection.

**Proof.** Put
\[
A=-C_s''(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{p^2e^{-2s(p^2+\omega^2)}}{p^2+\omega^2}\frac{dp}{2\pi}>0,
\qquad
B=\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{p^4e^{-2s(p^2+\omega^2)}}{p^2+\omega^2}\frac{dp}{2\pi}<\infty.
\]
Differentiation under the integral is justified by the Gaussian tail. All odd derivatives of \(C_s\) at zero vanish, and \(|C_s^{(4)}(t)|\le B\). Taylor's theorem therefore gives
\[
C_s(t)=C_s(0)-\frac A2t^2+R(t),\qquad |R(t)|\le\frac B{24}t^4.
\]
For \(F_h=\phi_s(h)-\phi_s(2h)\), whose two evaluation times are strictly positive,
\[
\mathbb E[(\theta F_h)F_h]
=C_s(2h)-2C_s(3h)+C_s(4h)
=-Ah^2+\mathcal R_h,
\]
where
\[
|\mathcal R_h|\le\frac{(2^4+2\cdot3^4+4^4)B}{24}h^4
=\frac{217B}{12}h^4.
\]
Choose any \(h>0\) with \(h^2\le6A/(217B)\). Then the reflected quadratic form is at most \(-Ah^2/2<0\). This violates reflection positivity.

If a formulation permits only smooth tests, replace each point evaluation by a unit-integral smooth function with sufficiently small support about the same positive time. Continuity of \(C_s\) makes the quadratic form converge to the strictly negative value above, so it remains negative. If only bounded observable tests are admitted, clip \(F_h\) to \(F_{h,M}=\max(-M,\min(M,F_h))\). These converge to \(F_h\) in ordinary Gaussian \(L^2\); reflection invariance and Cauchy–Schwarz imply convergence of their reflected quadratic forms. Thus a bounded positive-time test also violates the inequality. ∎

**Implication.** Reflection commutation and positivity of the underlying probability law are insufficient. Full-time smoothing does not preserve the positive-time observable algebra. One cannot insert the flowed observables directly into an OS reconstruction merely because the flow is gauge covariant and commutes with reflection. The example does not say that Yang–Mills gradient flow itself is ill-defined or useless; it isolates a missing implication in a proposed reconstruction route.

## 2. A support-preserving flow does preserve reflection positivity

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu,\theta)\) be a reflection-invariant probability space, with positive-half observable algebra \(\mathcal A_+\), such that
\(\int\overline{F\circ\theta}\,F\,d\mu\ge0\) for \(F\in\mathcal A_+\). Let \(W\) be a measurable map satisfying

1. \(W\theta=\theta W\);
2. \(F\circ W\in\mathcal A_+\) whenever \(F\in\mathcal A_+\).

Then the push-forward state \(\nu=W_*\mu\) is reflection positive on \(\mathcal A_+\), because
\[
\int\overline{F\circ\theta}\,F\,d\nu
=\int\overline{(F\circ W)\circ\theta}\,(F\circ W)\,d\mu\ge0.
\]
Both hypotheses are needed; Section 1 disproves the conclusion if only the first is retained.

On a finite lattice, spatial Wilson flow evolves spatial links on each Euclidean time slice using only spatial plaquettes of that slice. The defining vector field is smooth on a compact product of gauge groups, so its ODE has a unique global solution. It commutes with time reflection by uniqueness and reflection invariance of the spatial vector field. A spatial link in a positive-time slice evolves using only that slice. For observables generated by such links, hypothesis 2 holds exactly. Hence finite-lattice reflection positivity is preserved for these flowed slice observables.

This statement does not supply a continuum limit, a variance lower bound, a uniform temporal Dirichlet quotient or rotation invariance. It only avoids one obstruction of full-time smoothing. The flowed observable at fixed positive physical flow time also has a lattice support scale growing as the cutoff is removed; it is not automatically a fixed-stencil token of the manuscript's family definition.

## 3. Positive-time approximation and the error budget

Assume reflection invariance. For ordinary \(L^2(\mu)\) functions \(F,G\), define \(q(F)=\int\overline{F\circ\theta}F\,d\mu\). If \(\|F-G\|_2\le\varepsilon\), then
\[
|q(F)-q(G)|
\le\varepsilon(\|F\|_2+\|G\|_2)
\le\varepsilon(2\|F\|_2+\varepsilon).
\]
Indeed write the difference as the two terms obtained by changing one factor at a time; reflection is an isometry of ordinary \(L^2\), so Cauchy–Schwarz bounds them. Therefore, if \(G\in\mathcal A_+\),
\[
q(F)\ge-\varepsilon(2\|F\|_2+\varepsilon).
\]
For a sequence of approximate observables, exact positivity in the limit follows when the right-hand error tends to zero and the reflected correlations converge. An error bounded by a small fixed number does not suffice. In particular a localization estimate \(\varepsilon\le C e^{-c\delta^2/s}\) at fixed physical buffer \(\delta\) and fixed positive flow time \(s\) does not by itself tend to zero with lattice spacing.

If \(F,G\in\mathcal A_+\), let \([F]\) denote their OS classes. Then
\(\|[F]-[G]\|_{\rm OS}\le\|F-G\|_2\). For any contraction \(T\) of the reconstructed Hilbert space,
\[
|\langle[F],T[F]\rangle-\langle[G],T[G]\rangle|
\le\varepsilon(2\|[F]\|_{\rm OS}+\varepsilon).
\]
This gives an explicit requirement for transferring a retained two-point lower bound through a local approximation: the approximation error must be smaller than the claimed lower-bound margin. It does not establish that margin for Wilson theory.

## 4. Large-field removal requires a moment and rarity estimate together

Let \(B\) be a measurable bad event, \(q>2\), \(\|F\|_q\le M\), and \(\mu(B)\le p\). Hölder gives
\[
\|F\mathbf1_B\|_2\le M p^{1/2-1/q}.
\]
Thus rarity alone is sufficient only with a compatible moment bound. For the centered retained observable \(F-\mu F\) and its centered good-event truncation
\(F\mathbf1_{B^c}-\mu(F\mathbf1_{B^c})\), their ordinary \(L^2\) difference is
\(F\mathbf1_B-\mu(F\mathbf1_B)\), whose norm is at most \(\|F\mathbf1_B\|_2\). To preserve positive-time support, \(B\) itself must be measurable in the same positive-time algebra. A global bad event crossing the reflection plane does not automatically satisfy that requirement.

For a bounded observable \(|F_a|\le C a^{-d}\), suppose a fixed physical region contains at most \(C_Va^{-4}\) candidate bad blocks and each has probability at most \(C_Be^{-c\beta(a)}\). If
\(\beta(a)\ge\kappa\log(1/a)-B_0\), the union bound yields
\[
\mu(B_a)\le C_VC_Be^{cB_0}a^{c\kappa-4},
\quad
\|F_a\mathbf1_{B_a}\|_2
\le C(C_VC_Be^{cB_0})^{1/2}
 a^{(c\kappa-4)/2-d}.
\]
This particular bound tends to zero if \(c\kappa>4+2d\). At equality it gives only a bounded error, and below it it gives no vanishing error. This is a sufficient exponent budget, not a necessary condition on the real theory. The assumed per-block tail and uniform constants are themselves model-dependent obligations.

## 5. Correlation retention is equivalent to finite-energy spectral mass

Let \(H_a\ge0\), \(\psi_a\perp\Omega_a\), and \(\|\psi_a\|^2\le V\). Let
\(\nu_a(B)=\langle\psi_a,\mathbf1_B(H_a)\psi_a\rangle\). If, for one fixed physical time \(t>0\),
\[
C_a(t)=\langle\psi_a,e^{-tH_a}\psi_a\rangle\ge c>0,
\]
then, for every \(E>0\),
\[
\nu_a([0,E])\ge c-Ve^{-tE}.
\]
This follows by splitting the spectral integral into \([0,E]\) and \((E,\infty)\), and using \(e^{-t\lambda}\le1\) on the first set and \(\le e^{-tE}\) on the second. Choosing
\(E=t^{-1}\log(2V/c)\) gives \(\nu_a([0,E])\ge c/2\). Conversely, \(\nu_a([0,E])\ge\eta>0\) gives \(C_a(t)\ge\eta e^{-tE}\) for every \(t\ge0\).

Thus a fixed-time lower bound is exactly a uniform nonzero amount of bounded physical-energy spectral mass, provided total norm is uniformly bounded. The trial-state Dirichlet bound of manuscript Proposition `cl:prop:retention` is a sufficient way to produce this; it is not a necessary condition on the original unfiltered observable. For example, on a two-dimensional excited sector with
\[
\nu_a=\tfrac12\delta_1+\tfrac12\delta_{a^{-2}},
\qquad R_a=e^{-aH_a},
\]
one has \(C_a(t)\ge e^{-t}/2\), but
\[
a^{-1}\langle\psi_a,(I-R_a)\psi_a\rangle
=\frac{1-e^{-a}}{2a}+\frac{1-e^{-1/a}}{2a}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The spectral projection \(\mathbf1_{[0,E]}(H_a)\psi_a\) retains norm at least \(c/2\) and has energy quotient at most \(E\). It is a global Hilbert-space construction, not a local gauge-invariant field construction. The manuscript's positive-time semigroup filter gives another nonlocal retained vector, but neither procedure proves that an admissible Wilson observable initially has nonzero finite-energy spectral mass.

## 6. Published primary-source status

- Martin Lüscher, **Properties and uses of the Wilson flow in lattice QCD**, JHEP08(2010)071, DOI10.1007/JHEP08(2010)071. The publisher abstract describes theoretical and numerical evidence for smooth renormalized fields at positive flow time; it is not a construction of a nonperturbative four-dimensional continuum Yang–Mills theory. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08(2010)071).
- Martin Lüscher and Peter Weisz, **Perturbative analysis of the gradient flow in non-abelian gauge theories**, JHEP02(2011)051, DOI10.1007/JHEP02(2011)051. The theorem is all-orders perturbative finiteness after the underlying four-dimensional theory has been renormalized. It neither establishes convergence of perturbation theory nor constructs its nonperturbative probability law or mass gap. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP02(2011)051).
- Keita Sakai and Shoichi Sasaki, **Glueball spectroscopy in lattice QCD using gradient flow**, Phys.Rev.D107(2023)034510, DOI10.1103/PhysRevD.107.034510. SectionII distinguishes full space-time flow from spatial flow; it uses spatial flow to avoid temporal over-smearing in spectral measurements. Its numerical continuum extrapolations do not prove the uniform analytic Wilson estimates needed here. [Publisher](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.034510), [published open PDF](https://scoap3-prod-backend.s3.cern.ch/media/files/76059/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.034510.pdf).

The proof obligations left for the proposed route are therefore concrete: construct an admissible gauge-invariant observable with a uniform variance lower bound; retain nonzero bounded physical-energy spectral mass; control any localization/truncation error on positive-time observables; prove the required higher-correlation and continuum-axiom bounds; and separately prove the uniform weak-coupling cube datum. None is supplied just by perturbative flow finiteness.
