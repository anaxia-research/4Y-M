Title: The Yang-Mills Mass Gap Reduces to One Cube at Weak Coupling
Authors: Deep Bhattacharjee (corresponding), Ushashi Bhattacharya,
James Montgomery Lockwood, Tristan Romanov, Ikailo John Sessford,
Sanjeevan Singha Roy
Pages: 193 (13 sections, appendices A-I, 17 figures)
MSC 2020: Primary 81T25; Secondary 81T13, 82B20, 81T08, 47B65
Keywords: Yang-Mills theory, mass gap, lattice gauge theory

This is a revised version of manuscript 244356. It replaces that
manuscript: the results of 244356 are incorporated and extended in the
present text, which is self-complete. It continues our earlier paper
(Preprints.org 202611.1282, version 11), the only paper of
ours that it cites; the results it uses from that paper are restated in
Appendix I.

What it proves. For positive symmetric torus systems, a class that contains
Wilson lattice gauge theory for every compact gauge group, every coupling and
every positive-definite plaquette weight, one thermal inequality on one cube,
Z(c_T0, T0)^2 <= e^{1/4097} Z(c_T0, 2 T0), controls the free energy of every
larger box and gives a transfer-matrix gap of at least log(63/32)/T0 on every
cube of side at least 2 T0. Along a continuum family with a uniform
ultraviolet bound, or with a convergent continuum limit, the same datum at one
physical scale gives the limit a Hamiltonian with spectrum in
{0} union [Delta, infinity), Delta = t0^{-1} log(63/32), and a unique vacuum.
The inequality is proved at strong coupling: for every compact gauge group at
Wilson coupling beta_W <= 10^{-3} it holds with T0 = 12, so the lattice theory
has a transfer-matrix gap of at least (1/12) log(63/32) in lattice units on
every cube of side at least 24 (Section 8).

New in this version. Section 5 applies this to the Wilson family recently
constructed in Lean 4 (OpenAI, assessed by the Yang-Mills group on
10 November 2026), whose continuum limit is a mixture of Wightman phases.
The inequality along its tuning, on which the Wilson coupling grows like
log(1/a), gives the gap of the continuum limit and, by an averaging argument
over the phases, of every phase. For that family the four-dimensional mass
gap thus reduces to the one-cube inequality at weak coupling. Section 5 also
shows that a proof must use connectedness of the gauge group, and that the
inequality follows from a step-scaling bound at the asymptotic-freedom slope
(or its two-loop refinement), which is not proved.

Section 7 shows that along a family the inequality is equivalent to a gap
and a bounded free energy in the centre-neutral sector together with a decay
rate of 't Hooft's vortex weight bounded below in physical units. It gives
crossed versions of this criterion, shows that each gap is the exponential
decay rate of the corresponding free energy, reduces the thresholds to the
critical value of the zero-one law (the flux weight either keeps a limit
inferior above 8 x 10^{-5} or decays exponentially), and shows that the flux
weight is not monotone in the time extent (Z/2 at beta = 10). The neutral
condition holds if and only if the neutral gap is uniform and
liminf y^0(t)/t is below 1/54 of it.

Section 9 shows that on a fixed lattice the flux part of the defect exceeds
1.744 near the free point for every SU(N); that the vortex-rate route fails
when the box side grows faster than N^{3+delta} and for the heat-kernel U(1)
theory; gives a timed Cheeger bound for charged decorrelation at physical
times; and proves that for centre {+1,-1} and a vanishing neutral free energy
the datum is equivalent to the vanishing of the averaged overlap defect of two
slices, one of them flipped by the centre, at every ratio above 1/2 of their
time separation to half the side of the box. Section 9 also shows that
twists in one coordinate direction suffice, bounds the overlap defect by
Polyakov-loop correlators, expresses the flux gap through vortex weights in
physical units, and proves that for centre {+1,-1} the datum is equivalent
to a vanishing neutral free energy together with a bound
(2^488 * 8194^6)^{-1} on 't Hooft's vortex weight of the hypercube of
physical side 2t at arbitrarily large t. It proves the inequality of
Tomboulis and Yaffe in the slice limit, and shows that a heat-kernel U(1)
family at logarithmic coupling violates both halves of the neutral
condition. Section 12 adds the truncation of certified data, an ascent lemma
with errors, and a gapless generalized free field that has the ultraviolet
trace coefficient of the free gluon. The paper
also proves equivalent forms of the datum, the datum in two dimensions and in
a strong-coupling range uniform in N, and necessary conditions and
obstructions at weak coupling.

What it does not prove. The four-dimensional Yang-Mills mass gap is not
proved. The one-cube datum is not proved at any weak coupling along the
continuum tuning. Every continuum conclusion is conditional on it, and the
paper says so in each statement. The neutral-sector and vortex conditions of
Section 7, the overlap condition of Section 9 and the bound on the vortex
weight of the hypercube are not proved at weak coupling.

Verification. Every finite computation and exact constant is checked in
Lean 4 and Julia (exact rational arithmetic): 391 rigorous checks and 328
exported rational enclosures, recorded in Appendix H; the code is at
https://github.com/anaxia-research/4Y-M.
